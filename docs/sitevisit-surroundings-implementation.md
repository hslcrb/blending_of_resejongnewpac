# Surroundings reconstruction: sources and uncertainty

The expanded working scene is `public/model/sejong_surroundings_work.blend`. The original model remains backed up at `backups/blender/sejong_before_surroundings.blend`; an additional pre-excavation backup is `backups/blender/sejong_surroundings_before_sunken.blend`. See `sitevisit-photo-relationships.md` for the index of all 149 images.

The scene axes and coordinates are working approximations, not survey data: +X is approximately north, -X south, -Y east toward the plaza and carriageway. The pedestrian plaza, planting, watercourse and seven-lane carriageway are ordered according to Seoul municipal descriptions; the 60 m plaza width includes the pre-existing forecourt and is not added on top of it. The watercourse is shown as 212 m. Exact alignment with the building remains unverified.

The excavated sunken plaza is an interpretation of photographs 054–055, 060, 063 and 065. Their long display, glass guard and stair resemble Haechi Madang's 53 m screen, but the identification is not proven from the photos alone. Its X=-55 to -2 and Y=-108 to -94 coordinates, depth, stair, passage, ramp and dimensions other than the screen length are provisional. The media artwork and text were created from scratch. The lower floor and passage are at -3.25 m. Vertical geometry rays confirmed the lower floor, exterior paving, intermediate ramp height and carriageway are not covered by the extended foundation. Usable web walking heights and collisions still require separate integration testing.

The east streetscape expresses the official north-to-south sequence of National Museum of Korean Contemporary History, U.S. Embassy, KT West and Kyobo. KT's approximate gridded facade and twin screens refer to photo 013. Heights and spacing are provisional; museum and embassy are represented as eight-floor masonry masses; the Kyobo height is an approximate nonofficial figure. These masses are interpretations, not measured facades. The two original screen designs do not reproduce the photographs.

Plaza planting islands, trees, benches, and sidewalk lamps draw on repeated details in photos 001, 017, 066–091, 107–110 and 139; individual layouts are estimates. Photo 048 matches Starbucks Gwanghwamun at Sejong-daero 167; photo 075 depicts Mom's Touch LAB and Isaac at Sejong-daero 23-gil 3 and 5. Seoul city describes Sejong-daero 23-gil as the alley beside the center. Representative address points indicate that 23-gil 5 is about 36 m northwest of 167; they are not entrance measurements. Accordingly the independently modeled storefronts were moved to provisional south-alley positions, with centers roughly Blender (-118,-4) and (-94,22), about 35.4 m apart and preserving northwest order. Actual footprints, alignment, road shape and access are not surveyed. Original source studies remain separately preserved. See https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/1503961 and https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/18581071 . Covered passage 145 and arcade 148 have separate unplaced studies and are not asserted to connect to other spaces.

The image review files under `sitevisit-review/` are workbench studies, not final material-accurate photographs. Both day and night scene collections share the added surrounding geometry; The uncompressed candidate was 43.21 MiB because it lacked the existing EXT_meshopt_compression. The latest south-alley and gratitude-garden version is public/model/sejong_surroundings_verified_meshopt.glb, 24,098,480 bytes (22.982 MiB), with EXT_meshopt_compression enabled and EXP_FILTER_BITS temporarily set to 23 then restored. It is under the 25 MB asset limit. The latest candidate comparison against an otherwise identical uncompressed GLB decoded 544 meshes, 577 primitives, and 7,779,102 position components; maximum absolute difference was 0.00003052 m. All 11 embedded source-image SHA-256 hashes in that comparison match the prior GLB. Cafe/lightwell studies remain excluded from the main export. Browser appearance, loading performance, collision/navigation and deployment still require separate verification.

## Garden of Gratitude and northern watercourse

The separate Garden of Gratitude study contains 23 individually named 6.25 m granite sculptures in south-to-north order, each with a long horizontal foot curving concavely upward into a slim vertical section and an interpretive, separately excavated Freedom Hall entrance. The columns are approximately X=63..107, Y=-70; the entrance opening is X=110..122, Y=-67..-57. These are model coordinates, not surveyed real-world positions; the entrance geometry, stone colors, and internal space beyond its door are not established by the supplied photos. It is not the Haechi Madang media-wall study. The new entrance floor, steps and adjacent original paving were checked by downward rays.

The 212 m history watercourse is now approximately X=25..237 rather than centered on the site, representing the documented route from the northern Yukjo area to the south near Hangeul Fountain. This is a directional approximation; the fountain, statue reference position, exact course and edges have not been surveyed in this model. A pedestrian bridge was added at the road crossing.

Photograph-based and official references: https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2018143 ; https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2018125 ; https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2005281 . Reviews: gratitude_garden_eye.png, gratitude_freedom_entry_eye.png, surroundings_planted_top.png, plaza_planted_eye.png.

The current exported file contains 23 individual gratitude sculptures, separate Freedom entrance and Haechi plaza geometry, four additional shrub-layer meshes, the relocated shops, and no objects from the independent cafe interior study. The file requires EXT_meshopt_compression. The entrance contains no modeled Freedom Hall exhibition interior, and the King Sejong statue is not yet modeled.

The gratitude profile was corrected against the completed-work image in https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2018125 : parallel low horizontal beams, curved lower transitions, 23 tall sections, low warm linear lighting and top lenses. The 23 nodes share one mesh in the latest GLB. Their exact cross sections and locations remain approximate.

An independent statue study exists only in the Blender scene Photo study - King Sejong statue (unplaced), informed by the official 6.2 m figure and 4.2 m pedestal at https://news.seoul.go.kr/culture/design/public-archive?mpage=8&view=SS0002 . The first and tapered-pedestal views are king_sejong_statue_study.png and king_sejong_statue_study_refined.png. Its simplified face, clothing and throne do not resemble the original sculpture closely enough, so it has intentionally not been added to the site or exported GLB.

A second manual statue study integrates a continuous modeled face, narrower eyelids, beard, draped robe, crown, and throne details. Review images king_sejong_statue_hand_refined_front.png and king_sejong_statue_hand_refined_3q.png still show an overly stylized likeness. The study remains in its own scene, excluded from the site and public GLB pending a convincing comparison to the actual sculpture.

Photo 145 looks toward the temporary event installation on the main stairs also seen in photo 010, supported by https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISI20261027_0021506252 . The passage likely faces the top of the main staircase; precise coordinates, name and any connection to Haechi Madang remain unverified. Its independent model is retained rather than falsely attaching it to the sunken media wall. The separate day/night pavilion interiors are interpretive; photo 127 motivated an approximately 1.46 m opening in the long face with an approximately 0.23 m threshold. Door mechanism is not established by the photographs.
